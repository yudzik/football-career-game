'''FC Dinamo Minsk''' or '''FK''' '''Dynama Minsk''' ({{langx|be|ФК Дынама Мінск}}; {{langx|ru|link=no|ФК Динамо Минск}}) is a [[Belarus]]ian professional [[Association football|football]] club based in the capital city of [[Minsk]].

It was founded in 1927 as part of the Soviet [[Dynamo Sports Club|Dinamo Sports Society]], and was the only club from the [[Byelorussian Soviet Socialist Republic|Byelorussian SSR]] that competed in the [[Soviet Top League]], playing 39 of the 54 seasons, and winning the title in [[1982 Soviet Top League|1982]]. Since the independence of Belarus, the club participates in the [[Belarusian Premier League]], having won 9 league titles and 3 [[Belarusian Cup]]s.

Dinamo plays its home games in the 22,246-capacity [[Dinamo Stadium (Minsk)|Dinamo Stadium]] in Minsk. Dinamo is the second Belarusian team, after [[FC BATE Borisov|BATE Borisov]], to reach [[UEFA Europa League]] group stages ([[2014–15 UEFA Europa League|2014–15]] and [[2015–16 UEFA Europa League|2015–16]]).

== History ==
=== Soviet Union ===

Dinamo Minsk was founded in 1927 as a part of the Soviet [[Dynamo Sports Club|Dinamo Sports Society]]. They spent some of their history in the lower leagues of the Soviet Union, but in 1940, they were promoted to the [[Soviet Top League]], becoming the first and only Belarusian team to compete in the Soviet top division. They were relegated to the second level in 1952, but returned to the top level the next year. In 1954, they finished in the third place, their best performance in the top flight to date, and were dissolved, being re-founded as ''Spartak Minsk'', only to be renamed as ''Belarus Minsk'' in 1959, in honor of the Soviet republic in the national championship. However, in 1962, they returned to the original name of ''Dinamo Minsk''. They were relegated again from the top level in 1955 and in 1957. They played in the top level again in the 1960 season. They were relegated again in 1973 and returned to the top level in the 1975 season. But they were relegated immediately in 1976. They returned to the top level after 2 years. In November 1973, the team toured [[Bangladesh]] and played numerous exhibition matches against the regional selections.<ref>{{Cite web|title=Page 13; New Nation, 7 November 1973|url=https://eresources.nlb.gov.sg/newspapers/digitised/page/newnation19731107-1.1.13|language=Malay|access-date=26 January 2025|website=eresources.nlb.gov.sg|archive-date=26 January 2025|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250126160736/https://eresources.nlb.gov.sg/newspapers/digitised/page/newnation19731107-1.1.13|url-status=live}}</ref>

In 1982, Dinamo Minsk won the Soviet championship for the first and only time in their history. The following year saw them debuting in the [[European Cup]] against [[Grasshopper Club Zurich|Grasshopper]] of [[Switzerland]]. They reached the quarter-finals of the European Cup after eliminating Grasshoppers and [[Győri ETO FC|Győri ETO]] of [[Hungary]], only to be eliminated by [[FC Dinamo București|Dinamo București]]. In the 1984–85 season, Dinamo Minsk reached the quarter-finals of the [[UEFA Cup]] after beating [[Helsingin Jalkapalloklubi|HJK Helsinki]], [[Sporting CP]] and [[Widzew Łódź]], but were eventually stopped by [[FK Željezničar Sarajevo|Željezničar Sarajevo]]. 1988 saw Dinamo Minsk up to a new European performance, the quarter-finals of the [[UEFA Cup Winners' Cup]], passing through [[Gençlerbirliği S.K.|Gençlerbirliği]] and [[Real Sociedad]], but being eliminated by [[K.V. Mechelen|Mechelen]].

Dinamo Minsk also participated in [[Belarusian SSR]] league. Since the mid-50s, their appearances were only sporadic and they were represented by youth teams in later seasons. They have won the championship 7 times.<ref>{{cite web |title=Belarus - Championships before Independence |url=https://www.rsssf.org/tablesw/witrhist.html |access-date=6 October 2026 |website=RSSSF}}</ref>

=== Belarus ===
Dinamo Minsk won the inaugural season of the [[Belarusian Premier League]] in 1992. They won 5 league titles until 1995, making only one appearance in the [[UEFA Champions League]], in 1993. However, after a title in 1997, Dinamo Minsk last won the championship in 2004. The 2000s saw Dinamo Minsk failing to secure any league title in the battle against [[FC BATE Borisov|BATE Borisov]], thus finishing in lower places.

In 2014, Dinamo Minsk beat [[Myllykosken Pallo −47|MYPA]], [[CFR Cluj]] and [[C.D. Nacional|Nacional]] to be drawn in Group K of [[UEFA Europa League]], along with Italian side [[ACF Fiorentina|Fiorentina]], French team [[En Avant Guingamp|Guingamp]] and Greek side [[PAOK FC|PAOK]], becoming the second team, after BATE Borisov, to reach group stages of Europa League. Dinamo finished at the bottom with four points, after a draw with Guingamp and a historical 2–1 victory over Fiorentina.

=== Name history ===
* 1927, club founded as ''Dinamo Minsk'' as part of Dynamo sports society
* 1954, renamed to ''Spartak Minsk'' being transferred to Spartak volunteer sports society
* 1959, renamed to ''Belarus Minsk''
* 1962, renamed to ''Dinamo Minsk'' being transferred back to Dynamo sports society

== Supporters and Rivalries ==

The ultras of Dinamo Minsk are famous for their right-wing political orientation and there have been several riots, clashes with the police forces and chants against the Belarusian authoritarian regime, led by long-time President [[Alexander Lukashenko]].

Their political views as well as geographic proximity and contest for dominance of the city make them rivals with neighbours [[FC Partizan Minsk|Partizan Minsk]], whose fans tend to be strongly left-wing.<ref name="futbolgrad.com">{{cite web|url=http://futbolgrad.com/partizan-minsk-diy-football-club-from-belarus/|title=Partizan Minsk – the DIY Football Club from Belarus – Futbolgrad|date=13 August 2013|work=futbolgrad.com|access-date=8 February 2017|archive-date=22 December 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20151222171924/http://futbolgrad.com/partizan-minsk-diy-football-club-from-belarus/|url-status=dead}}</ref> Dinamo Minsk also has a big rivalry with [[FC BATE Borisov|BATE Borisov]] from the city of [[Barysaw]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.uefa.com/news-media/news/01ef-0f862621ffdb-7f8ea71f6e1f-1000--rivals-look-to-knock-bate-off-their-belarus-perch/|title=Rivals look to knock BATE off their Belarus perch|publisher=UEFA|date=31 March 2011}}</ref>

== Reserves ==
{{Main|FC Dinamo-93 Minsk|FC Dinamo-Juni Minsk|FC Dinamo-2 Minsk|FC Bereza-2010}}
There has been several teams that served as Dinamo Minsk official reserve or farm clubs.

*'''Dinamo-d Minsk''' was the club's reserve team which competed in the [[Soviet Top League]] (or [[Soviet First League|First League]]) Reserves championship. In 1992 this reserve team was transformed into '''Dinamo-2 Minsk''', which eventually got promoted to [[Belarusian Premier League]] and split into new club [[FC Dinamo-93 Minsk|Belarus Minsk]] (later renamed to more commonly known '''[[FC Dinamo-93 Minsk|Dinamo-93 Minsk]]'''). This club disbanded in 1998.
*'''[[FC Dinamo-Juni Minsk|Dinamo-Juni Minsk]]''' was formed as an outfit for young Dinamo players in 1993 and played in [[Belarusian Second League|Second League]] and [[Belarusian First League|First League]] from the 1993–94 season until the end of 2004. 
*New '''[[FC Dinamo-2 Minsk|Dinamo-2 Minsk]]''' was formed 2000. They were active in the [[Belarusian Second League|Second League]] during 2000–2002 and again in 2011–2012.
*'''[[FC Bereza-2010|Bereza-2010]]''' was originally an independent club, which since 2010 formed a partnership with Dinamo, serving as their farm club until the dissolution in late 2015.
*Since 2001 Dinamo is represented by a reserve team in [[Belarusian Premier League Reserves Championship]].
